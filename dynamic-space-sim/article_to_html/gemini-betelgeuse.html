Stellar Profile

Betelgeuse (α Orionis)

Colossal Red Supergiant: Betelgeuse is one of the largest and most luminous stars visible to the naked eye, forming the right shoulder of the constellation Orion. Although only ~10–14 million years old, its massive ~16.5–19 solar masses caused it to burn through its core hydrogen rapidly.

Giant Convective Cells: Unlike the Sun's millions of small convection granules, Betelgeuse's low surface gravity allows colossal boiling plasma plumes—some wider than Earth's entire orbit—to rise and sink across its surface, creating dramatic bright hotspots and cooler dark regions.

The Great Dimming & Supernova Fate: Between late 2019 and early 2020, Betelgeuse mysteriously dimmed by over 60% due to a massive convective surface mass ejection that cooled into a light-blocking dust cloud. Within the next 100,000 years, its core will collapse into a spectacular Type II supernova.
