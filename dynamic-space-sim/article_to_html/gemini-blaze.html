✦ Why Hasn't the Blaze Star Exploded Yet?

1. Binary Siphoning: T Coronae Borealis consists of an aging Red Giant bloated with hydrogen and a dead, ultra-dense White Dwarf orbiting every 227 days. Gravity pulls hydrogen off the Red Giant into an accretion disk around the White Dwarf.

2. Unpredictable Timing (~80 Years): Last erupting in 1866 and 1946, astronomers cannot directly measure the exact rate of gas accumulation or critical ignition mass—making the exact explosion date like forecasting the weather.

3. Thermonuclear Runaway: When pressure and heat reach a critical threshold, hydrogen fuses explosively across the White Dwarf's surface without destroying the star. Its brightness surges from +10.0 to +1.7—outshining Polaris—before fading over a week.
